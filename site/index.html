Where could grid-scale solar work on the Mendocino Coast?

A solar developer approached Caspar Water Company interested in a lease on its field near Highway 1. We learned that interconnection capacity is a major obstacle for new solar developments. The edge of the grid

If we want sustainable energy, we have to make it here.

The electric grid carries energy over transmission lines that separate major population centers. Our capacity to transmit energy to and from the coast is limited by the size of these lines. To bring a new solar utility online requires an interconnection agreement with PG&E. When grid is capacity constrained, transmission across that section is not permissible without upgrading the grid.

Transmission costs

Energy transmission consumes energy due to electrical resistance. Around 10% of electrical energy we use here becomes heat energy while crossing a transmission line. Energy costs more because we bring it in from away.

Legacy transmission lines

Coastal Mendocino is served by three aging transmission lines, designed before electric vehicles, residential heat pumps, and grid-scale solar. As energy usage grows, the coast will need new transmission lines or new local generation.

Backwards transmission restricted

PG&E's coastal power stations were not designed to feed power from the coast back into the grid. Energy generated here can't be sent away without first upgrading power stations.

Non-competitive market

According to Sonoma Clean Power, "The current grid was not planned with sufficient capacity to enable a competitive market." Because tranmission capacity is limited, energy has to be generated locally.

Local generation

The local high-voltage grid connects our former industrial sites.

The Mendocino Coast is served by a local distribution grid of 12kV power lines that links our towns. These lines were upgraded in the late 1940s when PG&E expanded service to a wider residential area and connected the local mills to phase out use of coal and kerosine. Locations with access to the 12kV local distribution network are ideal sites for solar generation.

Generation limits

Connecting a new solar generation facility to the energy grid shifts the dynamics of the grid. Electricity follows the path of least resistance and goes to serve the nearest demand. The farther energy travels the greater the waste; physical grid capacity means that generation is limited by local demand.

Integration capacity

Before connecting a new generation facility to the grid, the utility company must perform an analysis of the capacity for new energy production. CPUC requires energy utilities to publish Integration Capacity Analysis (ICA) numbers for sections of the grid to assist solar developers identify candidate sites.

Power control

Published ICA numbers are based on a variety of factors indicating how much power can be safely introduced without overloading the system. There are actually two ICA numbers, one for generation facilities and one for solar-only facilities. Because this study assumes battery storage, it uses generation ICA as the primary metric and reports solar-only ICA as secondary context.

Energy storage

The ICA number limits what a generation facility can produce at the peak, while sunshine limits energy generation to approximately 20% of the day in the Coastal zone. Adding battery storage enables a solar system to deliver energy around the clock. A site with 100kW generation ICA and battery storage can support a 500kW solar array.

Motivation

Why we made this site.

This site began as a question about land use. During the first attempt to develop solar on the Caspar Water company site, the Mendocino County Planning department discovered a discrepancy in its books. The County's General Plan section 20.376.015 says solar facilities can be built in Coastal Rural Residential zones, codified by Ordinance 3857 in May 1993. The Coastal Plan, which takes precedence, does not include this provision.

The Department has to weigh the impact of changing its list of Coastal Civic Conditional Uses for Rural Residential districts. Otherwise, the Coastal Plan only permits solar development in Agricultural, Forest Land, Timber Production, Rangeland, and Industrial zones. We ask, what would be the impact of this change? Would the Mendocino Coast be suddenly covered with solar panels?

Energy has limits

What we learned about solar on the Mendocino coast.

To develop a grid-scale solar facility on the Mendocino coast requires more than a Conditional Use permit with correct zoning. Successful development requires at least 10 acres of undeveloped land, access to the local high-voltage grid, that the local distribution grid has capacity, and that there is sufficient demand for electricity nearby. As soon as one generation facility is installed in a location, capacity and demand constraints will prevent another facility from being built nearby.

Candidate analysis

How were the candidates identified?

The study starts with all Mendocino APNs with at least 10 acres followed by exclusion and elimination for various reasons:

  • Sites more than 1km from PG&E's 12kV coastal distribution network
  • California prime, important, or unique farmland.
  • Protected areas including wetlands
  • Sites with improvement value above $10,000 per acre
  • Sections that slope more than 10 degrees
  • Open Space zoning.

Candidates were then ranked according to ICA number, distance from the distribution lines, and highway visibility.

Analysis

How many grid-scale solar generation facilities are there on the Mendocino coast? How many could there be?

There are no grid-scale solar projects on the Mendocino Coast, despite there being over seventy candidate sites east of Highway 1 with AG/FL/TP/RL/I zoning and available interconnection capacity. There are are 60 Rangeland, 7 Agricultural, 4 Forest Lands, 2 Timberland Production, and 1 Industrial parcel with compatible zoning.

Among the other zoning categories identified as candidates that are east of Highway 1 and with available interconnection capacity: 5 Rural Residential, 2 Remote Residential, 2 Public Facilities, and 5 in the Point Arena municipal zone.
